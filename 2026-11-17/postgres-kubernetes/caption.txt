🐘 Should you run PostgreSQL inside Kubernetes?

Your application runs on Kubernetes. Should its database move there too?

The answer depends on your requirements—and who will operate it when something fails.

🔎 What does the setup involve?

PostgreSQL manages your data. Kubernetes manages the workloads and storage resources. An operator such as CloudNativePG automates database lifecycle tasks, including provisioning and failover.

Your team still owns reliability, security and recovery.

🚀 Why consider it?

Platform teams want repeatable provisioning, GitOps workflows and infrastructure control across cloud and on-premises environments.

PostgreSQL on Kubernetes can support those goals when your team has both Kubernetes and database expertise.

A managed database is also worth evaluating, especially when reducing operational work matters more than infrastructure control.

⚠️ Consider a checkout database

You have one primary and two standbys across three availability zones.

If the primary fails, the operator can promote an eligible standby. Your application still needs to reconnect and safely handle interrupted transactions.

But what if someone accidentally deletes the orders table?

Replication can carry that deletion to the standbys.

Replicas help with availability. Backups help recover earlier data. You need both.

🛠️ How do we manage the risks?

• Spread instances across failure domains and test failover.
• Choose replication settings against acceptable data loss and latency.
• Keep protected backups outside the cluster and continuously archive WAL—the write-ahead log.
• Practise point-in-time recovery and validate the restored application.
• Monitor query latency, connections, replication lag, disk space and backup health.
• Apply least privilege, TLS, restricted networking and tested upgrade procedures.

Define your RPO—acceptable data loss—and RTO—acceptable recovery time. Then measure whether recovery drills meet them.
